## Background problem

Some parents and even some coaches at my club have an old school philosophy that you shouldn't focus on tactics, small sided games, until you have perfected the technical.  I dont follow that approach.  To me tactics means explaining overlaps, give & go, switching play, pressure/cover/balance.  And I dont see why you would play the game and not explain that.  I have a quarter of my practice to unopposed technical but the rest of son small sided games, rondos, and scrimmage usually geared towards a tactic, but of course they still get to learn the basics and I don't go too far in these topics, I teach them also at a basic level.    Also I talk about player positions and expectations for all positions (even though we dont focus on only one).  

My understanding is that my approach is highly recommended and closely mirrors the highly successful youth coaching philosophy used in the Netherlands.
The other coaches' belief that players must perfect their touch in isolation before learning basic tactics contradicts modern, world-class youth development models.

The Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB) actively opposes the idea of practicing techniques in isolation until they are "perfect". Your instinct that players can learn both technique and the big picture simultaneously is validated by the Dutch **TIC Model: Techniek (technique), Inzicht (game intelligence/insight), and Communicatie (communication)**. What you are calling "tactics"—give & go, switching play, overlaps, and balancing the field—is what the Dutch call developing "Game Intelligence" (Inzicht) and "Communication".
